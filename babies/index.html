In their own words

Tales of the
three fat babies

Three rescues, three very strong opinions. Pei runs the house, Moo ruins it, and Cata watches from somewhere high up.

Pei — six years old

Pei

The Queen · Expert Food Thief

I’m a six-year-old adopted girl who started my life in sunny Florida. Back then, I was a mama too — I had two litters of my own. But life was scary and full of unknowns, and I was so shy that Mama and Dad were really worried about me. Fast forward, and now I’m living my queen life!

After two years in Florida, I moved to Alabama with Mama, where I reunited with Dad. Mama spoils me rotten, and honestly, I only love her (sorry, not sorry). I’m an expert food thief and love hiking adventures with Mama, Dad, and my brother Moo. Life is paws-itively amazing!

Moo — four years old

Moo

Moo Moo Cow · Professional Nuisance

Hi, humans! I’m the chubbiest, cutest, and most amazing boy you’ll ever meet. I’m four years old now. Mama adopted me as a baby in Florida (same as my sisi), and she says she picked me because my cow spots are hilarious. So now she calls me Moo Moo Cow… all the time.

I love everything. I love chewing stuff, playing the dragging game, and most of all, annoying my sisters. Seriously, it’s my best talent! Sometimes my mischief gets Mama and Dad all mad, but honestly? Eh, I don’t care.

Cata — three years old

Cata

The Only Cat · Snuggly On Her Terms

Meow. I’m the only cat in the house, and I’m three years old. Life didn’t always look like this — I used to roam the streets near Dad’s old apartment. But one day, I saw Mama and Dad, and I knew they were my humans. And just like that, I found myself with a home!

Everything was new and strange at first. I had to share my space with a not-so-friendly sister dog and this huge, chunky, kind of scary dog. Let’s just say we didn’t hit it off right away. But now? We all live together peacefully.

I’m snuggly (on my terms, of course) and love my life, but let’s be clear — don’t get too close unless I say so!

Why we started

The day we noticed they were getting older.

One day, Sharon and I found a small bump on Pei. Around the same time we noticed more and more black dots on Moo — on his face, across his body. We did not remember either of them having those.

A little reading told us what it was. A sign of aging, nothing more. Our babies were getting older, and somehow we had not seen it happening.

Then, in 2026, friends of ours lost their pet — a member of their family in every way that counts. It made us ask a question we had been avoiding: what can you actually do to keep them with you?

This is our answer. Something with their face and their name on it, sitting in the room where they always sat. We hope what we make gives you the feeling that the ones you love are still right there.
